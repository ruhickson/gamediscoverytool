Farewell Bioware: The Impact of Mass Effect

On 21 October 2026, BioWare said the studio was closing, and that early work on the next Mass Effect was ending with it. Thirty years of Baldur’s Gate, Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age, and the Normandy, and then a full stop. This draft is the count of what Mass Effect left behind on Steam.

The same method as the Vampire Survivors note: pick a tag combination that describes the game people mean when they say the name, mark the dates that mattered, and see whether the storefront actually changed. No totals yet. The boxes below stay blank until that query has been run.

What this chart has to answer

Vampire Survivors had a clean before and after. Mass Effect does not. The first game is from 2007, the second from 2010, and Steam itself was still becoming the place PC games went to live. The useful comparison is the rate of this tag combination against the rate of all Steam releases over the same months, the same expected-growth line as the Survivors piece.

  • Count the cohort in the 24 months before Mass Effect 3, and the 24 months after.
  • Do the same around Andromeda, and around Legendary Edition.
  • Set each of those against the change in all games released on Steam, so a busier store is not mistaken for a Mass Effect effect.

Releases are not players

A month can ship twenty space RPGs and still be a rounding error next to one Mass Effect. The release chart says whether people copied the brief. The review chart says whether anyone showed up. Both are required before the word “impact” earns its place in the title.

Who the lane actually belongs to

Expect Mass Effect, the Legendary Edition, and Dragon Age to occupy the large tiles. The interesting tiles are the ones that are not BioWare: The Outer Worlds, Starfield, Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader, and whatever else the tags pull in. If those tiles are small, Mass Effect defined a desire that almost nobody else fulfilled. If they are large, the studio built a genre and then lost it.

Still to write

The closing argument waits on the series above. The facts that do not need a query are already plain enough to frame it. BioWare’s own account, posted on 21 October 2026, says the studio is closing and that early development on the next Mass Effect has ended. EA is concentrating on fewer, larger projects. The trilogy remains on Steam. The next one will not be made by the people who made the first four.

What the count still has to decide is whether that ending is also the ending of a kind of game, or only the ending of one studio’s claim on it. A recommender link for Mass Effect goes here once the piece is finished, the way the Survivors note points at its own game.
