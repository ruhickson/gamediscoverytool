The Shadow of Vampire Survivors: a trailblazing genre-lifting masterpiece

Four years ago, Italian developer poncle unleashed a game that took the industry by storm, their single-input Bullet Hell/Heaven game spawned not just a genre, but copy after copy, at a pace that seems to keep accelerating. With the advent of AI imitations of "new" games mopping up the Steam store being the meta of the moment, it's a good time take a look at the impact it has had on Steam historically, and how that effect continues to be felt today.

On the 20th of October 2022, Vampire Survivors launched after 10 months of Early Access and began to drug us all into a state of glorious addiction. I remember the first time I heard of the game was when my mate Colm said he was never installing it on his Steam Deck, lest it become a "Vampire Survivors" machine. How right he was. To understand just what a phenomenon was unfolding, I used a consolidated games database to track every game tagged with Action Roguelike, Pixel Graphics, and Bullet Hell on Steam from January 2020 through today.

Trend-setting

While the "Action Roguelike"-"Pixel Graphics"-"Bullet Hell" combination isn't unique to Vampire Survivors, or the genre in general, it's a pretty decent on-point description of the game, and likely the first game anyone would think if you asked them to name any game described by that.

The games industry loves following a trend, and it's clear that after the full release of Vampire Survivors, a new one had been set. Starting in January 2023, only three months after release, there have never been fewer than 12 Survivors-likes released in a calendar month, whereas before October 2022, there had never been more than 12.

This trend is still healthily growing, with the peak month of releases coming this year, in March 2026. Click on one of the bars to get a view of all the games released during that period!

  • 106 similar games were releasd in the 33 months before launch, roughly 3 a month.
  • 947 similar games were releasd in the 49 months since, closer to 19 a month.
  • There has been an increase in the games released per month across Steam in that time, with roughly 866 games released per month prior to October 2022. After, the average up to October 2026 is 1,640 per month. Esentially a 2x increase.
  • But amount of games matching the three tags identified has 6x'd over the same time. The gap between the expected growth (eSG) and this tag combination is quite vast in the lime dashboard line here.

Here's another way of looking at the same data. Each row is a year, each column a calendar month, and the intensity of gold shows how many similar games were released during that period. Vampire Survivors' early access and launch are indicated with the cyan and gold square boxes, respectively.

Steam really caught fire with this sort of game type since release. While the tag combo is still just an approximation of the Survivors-like genre, inspection of most of these games shows they are generally the same type of game: a top-down, slow-moving, levelling-up roguelike shooter.

  • 2020 and 2021 stayed under 30 releases a year. 2024 and 2025 each cleared 250, and 2026 is already over 200.
  • Though the second impact following the October 2022 launch is clearly bigger, it's worth noticing the first impact and uptick started within 3 months of the Early Access launch in December 2021.

Hail to the King

Presented as a share of all reviews of this type of game, it's clear to see Vampire Survivors is far beyond its peers in terms of popularity, with as many reviews as the next 20 most-reviewed games combined.

When a game achieves this level of virality and interest, it's natural that there will be a huge amount of homages, copycats, clones, cashgrabs, and genuine successors coming along, and the data backs that up.

  • Vampire Survivors has over a quarter of a million reviews reviews, 43% of the cohort total.
  • There are four other named games in this treemap alone with Survivor/s or Survival in their title
  • The other 1,018 games share 56,421 reviews: under 10% of the total review count for these tags, and almost all of the titles. It should be noted that the vast, vast majority of Steam titles get fewer than 10 reviews, so this isn't unexpected.

The Elephant in the Machine

Share of these games developed with AI

At the top, we did mention the "meta" around AI clones becoming more and more of a *thing* on Steam, the most obvious recent example being when Studio Bitdot brought attention to how their viral game Needle in a Haystack Simulator was cloned, likely using AI. And not just the concept, but the exact code itself has been identified in one of the now numerous knock-offs.

The debate over the use of ethical AI will not be explored in this article, however we're at the point where almost 40-50% of games on Steam are now developed using the technology in some capacity. The Survivors-likes are no exception, and we can see from the chart that more and more of them are being generated with AI, hovering in or around the 40% mark over the last 6 months.

Same band, same shape

Inside a given band the two groups look alike. In 11–1,000, which holds 3,928 of the 4,128 games with an AI disclosure, Very Positive accounts for 47% of their reviews and 51% of the reviews on games with no disclosure. Mostly Positive, Mixed, and Overwhelmingly Positive sit within a few points of each other too.

The AI side thins out quickly. Past 4,000 reviews a bar is often fewer than ten games, and past 9,000 it is often one title, so the stack stops being a mix. The 21 bands drawn here are every 1,000-review step from 11 through 21,000. Above that line there are 23 disclosed games and 469 others, and those 492 games hold about two thirds of all reviews in the window.

The gap is in the tags, and in a few huge games

Counted one game at a time, the positive rate is 79.5% with a disclosure and 80.9% without. Weight the same reviews by how many there are and the disclosed group falls to 74.3%, against 84.2%. That is not a worse score inside the same popularity band. It is because disclosed games rarely become the enormous, overwhelmingly positive hits, and because Call of Duty (717,339 reviews, Mixed, and an AI disclosure) is about a fifth of every review on a disclosed game.

Free and paid are the same shape: 20.9% of disclosed games are free, and 21.0% of the rest. The store has no dollar bracket on the game record, only Free, Paid, and a missing price (11 disclosed games and 188 others, Call of Duty among the 11).

Tags are where the catalog actually splits. Simulation is on 41.1% of disclosed games and 22.7% of the rest. Management, immersive sim, life sim, resource management, and economy lean the same way, and so does the Artificial Intelligence tag itself. Action, platformers, and hand-drawn games lean the other way.

Final thoughts

There are a number of stakes (hwehwehweh) in the ground here. The three-tag grouping is not infallible. As mentioned earlier, games like Neon Abyss are clearly not inspired by Vampire Survivors, and they are mostly two different styles of game, and this may be considered an inappropriate data point. Conversely, Brotato, a massively-popular game that came out in June 2023 and is undeniably a Survivors-like, is not included here because it is missing the "Pixel Graphics" tag. Considering the scale of games we're dealing with here, these data points do smooth out.


There is no definite answer on whether this imitative action is a good or bad thing, though I suspect it is on the whole a good thing. poncle's game is simple, lightweight, beautiful to look at, fun to play ("just one more turn... I can cheat Death this time!"). This is exactly the sort of thing that makes wannabe game devs believe they can make a game too, even if it's just copying what went before to learn how to do it.


Another thing the data shows is how incredibly rare a repeat success is when one game of a certain flavour captures the zeitgeist. Personally, I had never heard of HoloCure or Halls of Torment (the next most popular games in this category) before this analysis, but BALL x PIT and Megabonk definitely had their moments in the sun, the former on Twitch, and the latter on YouTube shorts.


Vampire Survivors created one hell of a ruckus when it launched, and there's no denying the impact it has had when we lay out all the games that followed and aped its style.


I think the really interesting part of all this analysis is looking to see if the same pattern happens elsewhere, and if it scales similarly. Survivors-likes grew 3 times faster (6x) than the expected rate of other types of games on Steam (2x) over the same date range.
