itch.io and the AI problem

Earlier this week, I posted about how October was the first month games with AI disclosure overtook games without one on Steam. Bear in mind that Steam releases are gated behind a $100 application fee and store QA review queue to act as a speed bump against automated publishing. But on itch.io the barrier to submission does not exist. This is not a criticism of its approach. itch.io is an incredible resource for aspiring developers to get their games published and playable. It just highlights that whatever challenges Steam has, itch will have even greater ones.

Between January and October 2026, generative AI usage on itch.io went from 17.6% to 56.4% across the platform.

In my article covering Steam's AI onslaught, we watched AI-disclosed releases reach over 50% share of monthly launches by October 2026. But Steam provides many hurdles for the developer to overcome, things like tax and banking verification, a $100 submission fee per title, and it must pass through manual build checks. What happens on a platform where publishing a playable browser game or asset pack takes seconds and costs nothing?

To find out, I began indexing the entirety of itch.io, though all that's needed to compare to Steam is what has been released so far in its 2026 catalog—496,929 storefront releases published between January 1 and October 31, 2026 (plus another 2,530 dead or purged pages already scrubbed from the servers). Also earlier this week, itch had a highly public removal of thousands of projects that didn't meet their standards and have begun to enforce more detail when it comes to what a project's AI disclosure entails. Unlike Steam’s single free-text disclosure box, itch.io asks creators a structured question when publishing: Does this project contain the output of Generative AI? If a creator answers yes, itch.io prompts them to check which parts were generated: Code, Graphics, Text, or Sounds.

The resulting dataset is interesting in how it gives much more resolution than Steam does. We'll take a look at that now.

Share of 2026 itch.io releases by AI disclosure status

What we see in the above graph is AI disclosure and explicit "No generative AI" disclosure (transparent) and "Undisclosed" (not-transparent). For the first seven months of 2026, non-AI releases comprised the majority on itch.io. In January 2026, out of 37,572 releases, 6,629 (17.6%) disclosed generative AI, while 23,624 (62.9%) explicitly declared “No generative AI was used” and 7,319 (19.5%) left the disclosure blank.

The number of games with AI disclosure was essentially growing at around 1,000 titles per month until July, when it grew to 2,000 extra per month. Alarmingly, between September and October, the number more than doubled.

Monthly AI-disclosed vs. Non-AI releases in 2026

The surge in growth is even more noticeable here. Look at the jump from September to October. The number of releases went from 23k to 52k. That is an untenable level of growth. Of course, most of these submissions were AI asset flips designed to flood and saturate the site in the hope of maximising monetisation, either directly or indirectly.

itch.io were swift in removing many terabytes of "ai slop", but the reality is not all ~30,000 new games were scythed from their pages. As already covered in the Steam article, a game being developed with the aid of AI doesn't necessarily condemn it to the "slop" pile. And many of the last two years' most popular and positively-reviewed titles used AI.

itch.io Creators' Primary Use Cases

Here is where we can get a taste of what could be. With a few simple tickboxes, the extent of what an AI disclosure should be is clear to see for everyone. Whereas on Steam, the declaration when it comes to the use of AI is binary, itch's solution is already visibly more powerful.

When looking at the Exact combinations, you can see Code-only usage is at 22.2% of all releases with AI disclosures. Anecdotally, I find this quite low, considering AI has been interwoven into IDEs for almost five years now, and it is inarguably a means to accelerate coding. Why I find it low is the multitude of loud voices online who claim all AI-assisted releases likely use it for code only and nothing else, but it's clear to see AI has been used for so much more than just the code explicitly, and 14.1% (almost 1/7th) of all projects use AI for Code, Graphics, Sounds, and in-game Text. This 14.1% would likely be considered vibe-coded by consensus.

Craft tools and AI growth

A worrying item from February this year came from Remi Verschelde, the lead maintainer of Godot, in how the PRs to their open source git repo were becoming overwhelmed with AI-generated content, making it a monumental task to review code that probably hasn't been looked at by those who submitted it. Looking at the growth in the usage of Godot, in both AI and non-AI instances, we can see where the exponentiality of the AI growth has come from. Godot has overtaken Unity as the #1 game development engine on itch.io, so when the AI growth scales with it, it scales faster than with any other technology.

The game engine with the highest percentage of AI-assisted releases is actually RPG Maker. Again, this is anecdotally interesting due to the streamlined and beginner-friendly manner in which RPG Maker games are formatted and scripted, which intuitively would suggest AI would not be a particularly in-demand asset here. And yet, the numbers are significant, with 26.8% of all games developed in RPG Maker using AI in some capacity.

Free vs. Paid: AI-disclosed uploads are more than twice as likely to charge money

About 1 in every 9 games with no AI disclosure require you to pay for them, whereas that number increases to about 1 in every 4 for those that do use AI.

It's hard not to think that there's some kind of "get rich quick" scheme being tested here when, despite making up the minority of titles, the number of games with AI disclosures that require you to pay actually outnumbers the number of paid non-AI games (37,878 vs. 37,801).

Initial price of paid 2026 itch.io releases (€1 to €30+)

Over 60% of all paid AI disclosure games are priced at under €5. There is a spike at €9.99 and, weirdly, more games are priced at €30+ than between €15 and €30. Go big or go home, I guess.

But are they any good? Game ratings:

As always, the player is the best judge of what's good. Just because a game uses AI doesn't mean it's bad, but it is a pretty strong indicator. The very best games on itch this year are overwhelmingly in the highest-scoring region: 4.75 - 5 stars - over half. Conversely, the quality split for games with AI disclosure comes around the 4.25 stars mark, with half of all AI-developed games falling above and below that mark.

I don't want to dwell too much here because what I wrote on Monday still applies here, just to add that itch's approach is already notably healthier than Steam's when it comes to the handling of AI. There is no simple fix for any of this, no silver bullet that works for everyone, but this is about as good as a first step can be.

And if you are a gamer, trust yourself - gamers are clearly much more discerning when it comes to the quality of these products than the platforms themselves or any algorithm could ever be.
